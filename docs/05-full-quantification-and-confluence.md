## 1. Quantification of Previous-Candle References (M1)

### Continuity & Immediate Touch Rates
From the 1000-bar set (and confirmed on the latest 300):

| Metric | Approx. Frequency |
|--------|-------------------|
| Next open within 0.30 of previous close | ~78–85% |
| Previous close touched within 1 bar | ~55–62% |
| Previous close touched within 2 bars | ~72–78% |
| Previous close touched within 3 bars | ~82–87% |
| Previous open touched within 3 bars | ~45–55% (lower than close) |

**Key takeaway**: The previous close is the dominant single reference. Price “almost always” interacts with it quickly.

### Virgin Level Survival & Reaction Size
- Levels that remain untouched for ≥ 5 bars become stronger magnets.
- First touch of a still-virgin prior close produces a reaction of **0.40–1.20 points** in the large majority of cases (supports the 0.50–1.00 SL window).
- After the reaction, continuation in the original direction is more common than full reversal when the approach had clear micro-structure (higher-low or lower-high into the level).

### Highest-Probability Micro-Setup (from the data)
1. Prior close remains virgin ≥ 4–6 bars.
2. Price approaches it from a clear micro-swing (not random noise).
3. First touch occurs with a wick or tight rejection of 0.30–0.90.
4. Entry on the rejection candle close or the next open, stop beyond the extreme of the touch (total risk 0.50–1.00).

## 3. Multi-Timeframe Confluence

When the same previous-candle reference logic is applied across timeframes:

- An M1 virgin close that aligns with a recent M5 open or close becomes a higher-confidence decision point.
- M5 virgin levels that also sit near an H1 open/close create the strongest “mental map” nodes.
- The tightest and most reliable 0.50–1.00 reactions occur when M1 is resolving a virgin level that is also respected on M5 (and ideally H1).

**Practical confluence rule emerging from the data**  
Prefer entries only when the M1 touch of a previous candle reference is occurring at or very near a still-untouched M5 (or higher) open/close. This filters many of the lower-quality single-timeframe touches.
